# Portfolio Hub Usability Pilot Kit

## Purpose and boundary

This kit prepares a small, qualitative usability check of the Portfolio Hub and its five-minute review path. It is not a completed study, does not establish hiring outcomes or product-market fit, and should not be described as external validation until sessions have been run, recorded, and summarized.

Use it to learn whether a recruiter, hiring manager, or technical reviewer can quickly identify the strongest evidence, understand the scope boundaries, and find the source or live demo they want to inspect.

## Recommended study shape

- Participants: 5–7 people who review technical work as part of hiring, product, engineering, or operations work.
- Format: 20–25 minute remote screen-share or in-person session.
- Materials: the current production Portfolio Hub, a note-taking sheet, and this guide.
- Capture: observations and verbatim feedback only with the participant's permission. Do not collect sensitive employment, health, financial, or account information.

Recruit participants using a neutral invitation: “I am testing whether a technical portfolio is easy to review. This is a short usability session, not an interview or an assessment of you. Would you be willing to share what is clear or confusing while you review it?”

## Moderator opening script

“Thanks for helping. I am testing the portfolio, not you. Please think aloud as you go. There are no right answers. With your permission, I will take notes about what is clear, confusing, or missing. You can skip any question or stop at any time. This session is not connected to a job application or employment decision.”

Ask for explicit permission before recording. If the participant declines, take anonymized notes only.

## Tasks

1. Starting at the Portfolio Hub, find the work you would inspect first for an applied-AI or technical-operations role. Explain why.
2. Use the five-minute review path. Tell the moderator what each project demonstrates and where you would look for deeper evidence.
3. Find the boundary between a live, public demo and a local-only application.
4. Locate one source repository or case study and explain whether you trust it as the source of authority.
5. Use the catalog filters to find a commercial product and then return to the employer-facing work.
6. Identify anything that could be mistaken for a claim about customers, revenue, adoption, certification, or compliance.

Do not help unless the participant is blocked for about 30 seconds. Record the point of uncertainty before offering a prompt.

## Debrief questions

1. What would you remember after closing the portfolio?
2. Was the first review action obvious? If not, what did you expect instead?
3. Which evidence felt strongest, weakest, or hardest to verify?
4. Did any wording feel overstated or ambiguous?
5. What one change would make this easier to review in a hiring context?

## Synthesis and publication guardrails

After all sessions, group observations by recurring issue, not by individual. Mark each finding as **observed**, **inferred**, or **not yet validated**. Prioritize a change only when it has a clear user impact and can be tested again.

Before publishing any outcome, remove names, employers, screen recordings, contact details, and identifying quotes unless separately authorized. A suitable public summary says how many sessions were run, the participant mix at a high level, the tasks, major recurring findings, and what changed. It must not claim statistically representative results, hiring validation, customer demand, or outcomes that the sessions did not measure.
